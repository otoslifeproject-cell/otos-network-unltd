Box 3.11,971 / 2,000
Tell us about yourself and what led you to start your social venture.

I drank daily from fifteen. For twenty-five years it was not a problem. It was solving one I did not know I had. ADHD was not a thing to me. Drinking gave me focus, function, confidence, motivation and lifted a morning fog I could not name.

At about forty I crossed the invisible line into dependency. I wanted out. But every attempt to stop drinking also took away my ADHD medication — the one thing holding the condition underneath. You cannot go one way and you cannot go the other. People like me are trapped right there.

I was diagnosed privately in November 2020, funded by my parents. It still appears on no patient-facing record — not the NHS App, not MyChart — after a surgery change lost my history. The ADHD team hold it. Nobody else was told.

I have been on an NHS medication-review list since the Shared Care Agreement of 21 January 2021. That agreement was lost with the surgery change. Five years on I am still unreviewed with no shared care in place and unable to register elsewhere because my diagnosis is private, not Right to Choose.

In those five years: six detoxes, repeated A&E, two ICU admissions, a suicide attempt, residential rehab, arrests and courts. Five colours of crisis, five records, none talking to the others. Every service did its job. Nobody held the thread, and nobody asked what was underneath.

In April 2026 an independent consultant psychiatrist wrote what nobody had connected: "adult ADHD, previously complicated with excess alcohol use to self-medicate inadequately managed ADHD symptoms."

The correct treatment was life-changing and immediate. I have worked twelve-hour days since, decorated my flat, committed to the gym.

I am not angry at anyone. Everyone was doing their job but significantly, on my final, engineered detox, the alcohol liaison nurse said she would appreciate "more places to signpost" as she had "nowhere left to send me". I said, "next time we meet, I won't be your patient, I'll be your partner."

Section 6 · Impact & inclusion

Box 6.11,996 / 2,000
What is the social or environmental issue you are addressing?

Suspected ADHD-driven addiction. The system treats the crisis. Not always the condition driving it.

For a significant and identifiable group of adults, addiction is not the whole clinical picture. The visible crisis is alcohol, relapse, detox, A&E. The driver underneath is untreated or inadequately treated ADHD. The system saw what was on fire. It did not ask what was burning.

And the wait has a consequence: repeated long enough, self-medication crosses an invisible line and the body physically requires it. Stopping the drinking removes the only regulation there is. Diagnosed, rehab often removes the ADHD medication at the door because it is a stimulant. People cycle through revolving service doors.

NICE NG87 (rec 1.4.3) names "the increased risk of substance misuse and self-medication". The Royal College of Psychiatrists' CR235 states that substance misuse "may be twice as common among individuals with ADHD…and tends to begin earlier".

The scale is published: up to 803,658 referrals that may be for an ADHD assessment in England, March 2026 (NHS England Digital, 28 May 2026), a count NHS England says under-represents the true number. The independent ADHD Taskforce puts the avoidable cost of unsupported ADHD at £17 billion or more a year.

Locally, 4,690 adults are in structured addiction treatment in Cambridgeshire and Peterborough (NDTMS 2024/25), growth +12.3% against a national +6%.

Every service owns its own episode. Nobody owns the time between them. It is not a gap in care — it is unowned risk. A referral is not proof that a connection happened. A discharge plan is not proof that the next step landed. A waiting list is not a holding layer.

So people wait with unmanaged ADHD, then reappear — in A&E, in addiction services, in crisis teams, in detox, in rehab, in housing pressure, in police contact, in mental-health crisis, sometimes in prison. By the time the system catches up, the original need has become much more expensive and much harder to unwind.

Box 6.21,981 / 2,000
How do you plan to tackle this social or environmental issue?

The intervention is clinical and belongs to clinicians. OTOS surfaces the communications between a person and the services around them, so the intervention can be reached and can hold.

OTOS Continuity is a shared Layer between the person and their services — a protective ring around them, not a system above them. Each service has its own interface on the Layer, delivering and receiving. Every existing connection between service and person continues separately, unaffected. Nothing changes in how existing services work.

People are held and contacted daily and automatically instead of fortnightly calls and official appointments. "Micro Check-ins", including recovery content, aid keyworkers whose time is scarce, increase connection, re-enforce new behaviours and maintain continuity of service in the risky gap.

A check-in is not surveillance, it is a heartbeat: the person is still there, so care continues quietly. Outwards, the same thread makes drift visible, records referrals, confirms delivery and logs attendance, so a handoff is confirmed, not assumed.

The Layer carries many individual Threads, with authorised partner views and separate service records. No clinical record access is needed, so it can be tested in months.

In practice it is a chain that does not exist yet: Alcohol liaison meets someone for the third time and refers them onto the Layer. The ADHD service picks it up with context attached: a high-intensity user of urgent care, not a routine referral. The drug and alcohol service is notified. Active waiting starts at once, not after a diagnosis. Assessment and titration begin and addiction work restarts knowing the condition underneath is treated.

Care happens in moments. Continuity happens in the days between.

This award does not build the product; it builds the case that makes it fundable: months 1–3 the local evidence base; 4–6 the governance pack that lets a service say yes; 7–12 insurance, agreements and a costed demonstrator.

What is/will be the impact of your work?

Short term, this award funds no service: it funds a local evidence base, a governance pack and a costed demonstrator — what a service needs to agree.

Then the test: up to 50 adults, 12 weeks active, read at 3 and 6 months, where consent and governance allow. Decision-grade local evidence, not a rollout, not proof. START (PMID 41218347; French, retrospective, no control group): 61.3% showed improvement in addictive disorders six months after ADHD diagnosis. Twelve weeks is a residential rehab stay; this mirrors it and adds the medication mine was missing.

People are contacted at least daily, automatically, and the thread does not stop at discharge — where one human follow-up could prevent catastrophic harm.

Services reasonably respond to the addiction. But if the ADHD remains untouched, the work does not hold. Therapy, groups and keywork land on a brain that cannot sequence or retain them. People are asked to recover using the exact executive functions their unmanaged ADHD has impaired.

The sequence is the argument: treat the ADHD driver, then hold both pathways long enough for recovery to land. The addiction work becomes usable and cost should fall. Continuity alone does not do that; medicine plus continuity is what is tested.

This is not new cost — the old cost doesn't disappear: it is a smarter use of what the system already spends.

NHS England's ADHD Taskforce says ADHD care "should be seamless" with "a single, accessible front door" — the impact I am aiming at.

Partner burden is measured as a pre-pilot success criterion: if it takes too long, we have failed. The read-outs and the simplest questions: did they have ADHD? did they get out of addiction faster? are they relapsing?

Not every addiction is ADHD. But for a real, high-cost, high-risk cohort the system may be treating addiction crisis while the deeper cause remains unresolved — and until it is, it repeats.

This is not just for the person trapped and suffering; it is for everyone who loves them.

Box 6.71,912 / 2,000
How will you ensure that your work is accessible and inclusive?

The people this is for are already inside services. They do not need finding. They need surgically targeting through the services they are already in, not advertising. I can describe exactly who: adults in treatment for alcohol or drugs whose ADHD is suspected, unconfirmed, awaiting diagnosis, sitting on a medication review list or in titration already.

Accessibility is the product, not a feature of it. The whole design assumes the user has impaired working memory, difficulty initiating and no reliable felt sense of future time — because that is the condition it serves. For that brain, "ring this number next week" may not exist as a felt concept. It is built for the brain it serves, not the brain services see.

Reaching them is not the same as holding them. People with addiction and untreated ADHD do not disengage loudly. They go quiet but turn up again in a new crisis. That is the gap OTOS Continuity is trying to close.

On involving people in the design, services contribute as little or as much as they see fit and set their own input levels rather than being handed a specification. Examples being, the alcohol liaison nurse needs one "refer to Continuity" button on a device and nothing else. The recovery education service would carry far more, because it is where the ADHD team send you to wait and learn. OTOS can put that learning in the participant's hands. The ADHD team decide what statuses they post, and nothing more is asked of them. It is built for minimum extra effort from clinicians, nurses and keyworkers and nothing is agreed with any of them yet.

On the people who would use it, I will be straight. Nothing formal exists yet. What exists is that I would have been one of them. I needed this kind of attention five years ago. But it cannot rest on one person. I cannot describe participants I have spoken to, because there are none and I would rather say so than invent them.

Section 7 · Sustainability

Box 7.11,998 / 2,000
How do you plan to generate income to keep your social venture going?

I want to be straight: I have no revenue, no contract and no funding secured. The company exists as a vehicle, something that can hold the work and its value, and be invested in or contracted with when there is something to contract for.

Five routes, and they fund different things, which matters:

Research funding funds the study rather than the company — a research team testing whether the thesis holds. That is the primary route I am pursuing and it needs an academic principal investigator, which I do not yet have, though one is in sight.

Local commissioning funds the service where it is delivered. In Cambridgeshire the £47.8m adult drug and alcohol contract has recently been re-procured and awarded, so there is no procurement route in. The realistic route is redirection of spend happening now, through local public health commissioning and that is a live conversation rather than a hypothetical one.

Investment would fund the company itself, in exchange for equity. That route is open and a colleague leads it.

The fifth I am testing rather than relying on. The East of England Clinical Digital Health Solutions Framework runs to September 2028 and is free for any NHS body to use. New suppliers cannot join mid-term, but those already on it can add products to the catalogue, so licensing through an existing supplier could reach NHS bodies across the region without a new procurement. I have no evidence on how often that happens, so I treat it as worth testing, not as a route known to work.

Grants like this one fund the founder and the groundwork, which is the stage I am at.

The eventual model is a licensed Continuity infrastructure paid for by the organisations that benefit from people staying connected — commissioners and providers, not the individuals using it. Nobody in this position should ever be asked to pay for Continuity. I do not yet have proof of demand at a price and I am not going to claim I do. Establishing that is part of what the demonstrator is for.
